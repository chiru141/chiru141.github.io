AI has complicated the life of consultants and not in a way anyone would imagine. 

We can use AI and save time on a lot of research work and create slides using a hybrid combination of various LLM tools available in the market. It makes us efficient, however, when we do that, our leaders tell us that they are "bored with the claude html outputs, and let's bring a bit of traditional consulting slides back to show our clients."

and then there's some research or analysis work which these LLM models have not yet been able to master. They are always making lame mistakes and we constantly find it easier to just do it ourselves. That's when our leaders need us to show that we are efficiently able to use the LLMs to be more efficient. 

When a writer would write articles by themselves in a pre-LLM model era, the readers would irk at the missed apostrophe in the word "its" hidden in the fourth sentence of the third paragraph. Now the readers find themselves irking at the impeccable grammar and the long hyphens that any LLM would add when asked to do a grammar check on any article. I think the writers have probably intentionally started leaving a mistake or two, to make sure their readers think it's not LLM generated. 

Why do we feel the need to differentiate ourselves from the crowd? why do we always have to want things differently than what others are doing to "stand out" and then also want to feel the same things. We want to *be like everyone*, but not like *everyone*. We want to wear similar clothes at any event but we also want to make a statement. We want to be different, but don't feel like an outcast. 

So, do we want to be different or do we want to be same? why do we have a problem with everything these days?
