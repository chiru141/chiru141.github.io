We have a debate session scheduled for this weekend with the public policy course I am enrolled in. 

While all of them are worth debating, one of the questions piqued my curiosity first: **Should Social media be banned or regulated for children under the age of 16?** 

Now, my question is: are we planning to debate if social media is bad for children under the age of 16? or if it should be banning or regulating is the right approach to protect the children from the potential harm? 

I think most people would agree that Social media is not good, not just for children under the age of 16, but for anyone who's spending more than a few hours on it. **Concerns around harmful content like online pornography to fraud & exploitation to internet addiction** [2] are based in real experiences across the world. Many studies show that social media **affects mental health** and has led to an increase of anxiety, depression and other mental challenges in children and adults alike. [8]

So, I believe the **question is NOT if the social media should be accessible for children aged 16 but it is if it should be banned** or regulated. 

An **outright ban** as noted by several research and policy agencies would be **counterproductive**. why? 

Because **How would the Governments enforce it?** Making it a responsibility of Social media platforms to not allow children under 16 to create accounts and fining them in case of violation is one way to go with it. Australia announced this in December 2025 and 4.7 Million under-age accounts were taken down. Australia guided **platforms to use facial age estimation features** to identify children under the age of 16. Malaysia announced the ban in 2026 and decided to use **National identification Authentication Systems** to provide age proofs, and logins.  [1][2][8] 

However, These methods raise **concerns around collection of highly sensitive information** and data privacy. Platforms/ Governments collecting user data could be used for profiling, online targeting and leak leading to increased child safety and **security concerns.** [1][2]

Even after that, the ban might not successfully keep children off of social media. 3 months after Australia announced the ban, it was reported that 8 out of 10 children were still accessing social media by **manipulating their age during registration, bypassing facial recognition** based age verification systems by photo manipulation software, celebrity photos, clever make-up or utilizing older friends / parents. It was also found that they were **using VPNs to bypass age based restrictions.** In some cases, parents were helping them access the platforms. [4]

Also, some policymakers worry that total bans could push kids to **migrate to even riskier online** **spaces** which is not being monitored and would be away from eyes of parents and guardians leading to increased risks. [3]

Also unlike the much known addictive and potentially harmful effects, there are **benefits to social media** as well, especially for teens who feel disconnected from their local communities and want to build relationships with others who feel similarly. [3] Many parents want to provide access to their kids - responsible access to social media to help them connect with like-minded peers, find information content, and learn digital safety skills. [4] This is the reason their are parents who provide their kids with ways to access social media under supervision. After all of this, there's the case of violation of freedom of Expression. France tried the ban, but the high court denied it on grounds of violation.

So, then what is the alternative of a blanked ban on Social Media? 

In the research conducted by MIT Technology [4], they found that parents and kids both want smarter, more nuanced policies to protect children, families, and their rights. Rather than locking kids out entirely, effective policy could take the form of:

1) **Safety-by-design mandates** for strict platform defaults to address infinite scrolling, auto-play loops and behavioral engagement platforms.

2) **Flexible Parental Tools** to control content and access. 

3) **Stronger limits on data collection** and targeted advertising directed at Children

4) As proposed by *The Conversation*, [3] complementing online safety with **digital literacy and healthy screen-free time.** 

As India discusses the idea to regulate social media and tries to balance policy enforcements, right to expression, data security concerns and parental control along with child safety and future, I hope it takes into account the learnings from other countries and looks beyond a blanket ban, and advocates **regulated social media access through strategic system design.**
