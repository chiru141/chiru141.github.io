I have been learning more about *Rama*, yes, the hero from the Hindu epic Ramayana. 

While growing up, like other girls my age, I struggled to understand why they would celebrate Rama-Sita as a couple. Why do we play the Rama-Sita hymns and conduct the Varmala ceremony *(which will follow from Rama-Sita's version of marriage ceremony)*, if their story ends in heartbreak?  A few others and I argued if Rama was a good husband. Good son, sure, good king of course. a good husband? Probably Not. 

How could anyone think he was a good partner when he wouldn't accept his wife after fighting the war with Ravana, because of citizens' whispers? Sita was regarded as the literal epitome of purity in the epic. She endured fourteen years of harsh forest exile by his side, only to face public trial and abandonment. Even having a God as her partner didn't save her from the betrayal and hurt.

This became a common conversation with other girl friends in the various weddings I attended last season, and I started thinking about it. Why would people celebrate Rama-Sita's marriage ritual if they didn't think that was not love and it was not something to aspire for. 
I started reading a bit more about Rama, thinking more about him, as a character in an epic, as a human, as a king, and as "God"? Compared him to Krishna, Lord Vishnu's another avataar, quite different from Rama avataar. Tried to understand krishna, how he loved Radha, and married Rukhmini and 7 others. *(of course 16000 but that was different)*. Rama had only one woman in his life. 

That hit me. It was Rama who chose monogamy in an era where polygamy was absolute norm especially for a king. That was an act of devotion to his wife, his partner and his love. Rama never remarried even after he exiled Sita to honor the wishes of his citizens. He chose to live out his life as an ascetic. 

Till now, I was used to thinking of him as a King, as an avataar of Lord Vishnu. An avataar who was "Maryada Purushottam", never left the side of dharma, a rigid character in the story who would never change his rules even when he thinks he is right. 

But maybe I got it wrong. He was a romantic at heart. His character was more nuanced than the identity of Dharma Follower and protector that was provided to him to guide generations of Indians. He was probably emotionally much more softer and kinder than I would have imagined him, and Sita was his strength. 

Sita was able to embody her femininity because of Rama's absolute protection. 

Sita was never passive or weak, as it probably feels as one goes through the narrative. she was a fiercely strong, independent woman. Her immense Tejas and boundaries were so potent that Ravana despite holding her captive was never able to physically violate her space or break her spirit.  But it was the certainty of Ram’s unyielding protection that allowed her the emotional safety to let her femininity shine. 

Now, I agree that most of my knowledge about Ramayana and the various characters are from teachings from my elders, different stories I read on the internet, and retellings I watched on TV. I have never yet fully read the text myself. I wonder if reading the text in its entirety would help me delve deeper into the nuances of moral and emotional battles that Rama fought with himself.
