*Note on macro-anxiety, vanity metrics, and the lateral leadership we actually need.*

Rupee is falling, and if you follow all of the other macro-economic pressure indicators and conversations: one of the conclusions everybody reaches is that India is not innovating enough, that we are still being used as cheap labour. 

This after government is spending thousands of crores in developing startups and a significant number of 20 year olds are taking the start-up plunge. Over 2.23 lakh startups have been registered and recognised, with thousands added just in the last 12 months. 

Is the competence that is lacking? I don't think so. 
Direction may be? I think I meet enough people and talk to them enough to say that they are all smart people, driven even, but none of them including me can decide where our energies are best put. We have ideas, we work on them, but nothing or very little comes out of it. 

Some of us feel like we have already failed cause we didn't make the 30 under 30 list or didn't build the next big thing. Leave all of that, we wonder if we will be able to achieve what our parents did. Building steady foundations with far fewer resources. As a teenager it seemed all so easy. What happened to that potential? May be the world was lying to us, may be we weren't good for anything. 

I think what everyone is lacking is leadership, I know people use it as a joke, but Covid especially has left everyone confused . Should we think for Long term or short term? We need time to build but it's always running out. We look at problems top down, but the bottom up seem too small to solve for. 

We seek mentors. We seek direction and leadership. Constantly looking for people who can advise us on what to do and if we do get this advice, it doesn't feel right for a constantly changing future. We feel the world is too uncertain. We are always consuming the doomsday scenario. we are always thinking that there's some one already doing what we are thinking of doing, that we have already lost before even starting. 

I think it's time to pause. Time to collaborate instead of compete, to put our heads down and work on one small, deliberate step at a time. We don't need to conquer the world by thirty. We just need to realize that macro-volatility has always been the norm, we just consume more data about it now. Generations before us figured it out in the dark, and we will too.
