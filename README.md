Most of us introduce ourselves like this - Engineer, VIT Vellore | Cisco | IIMB | Accenture Strategy 

I think we do that because we misinterpret that famous line from Batman:

*"It's not who I am underneath, but what I do that defines me"*

But I align more with the philoshphy that **we are not our jobs, its just something we do.**

And hence, when somebody asks me to introduce myself, I fumble. 
I think about the various ways I could describe myself and then I try to reason if those are just things I do, roles I play or that is actually who I am. By the time I am done thinking, the person in front of me has already judged me and left. 
After over 100+ introductions in my life, I have found a better approach. I only share the specific pieces of my work, experience or hobbies that I think the person would actually connect with.

But how do you do that on a static blog page? Relying on rigid job titles or listing one or two isolated hobbies feels incredibly limiting.

My name is Ruchi, and it means *Liking, interest, inclination*, often used to describe hobbies or in professional settings an interest area. True to my name, I find myself having *ruchi* in various subjects - a lot of times seemingly unrelated. 

I have always owned multiple diaries and jotted down my ideas on various note-taking apps, occasionaly publised an article or two for college club newsletters or internal company websites. 

For a long time, I resisted writing publicly online. It felt like adding to the noise. In a world full of experts, it’s easy to convince yourself that someone else has already written about a topic better than you ever could.

Then, I came across blogs where the authors shared their incomplete thoughts. This idea of sharing unpolished and yet-to-be-completed thoughts spoke to me. These digital spaces are not exactly trying to teach, instead, they introduce the author's current curiosity curve to invite dialogue from other people who are thinking, researching, building or writing about similar things.

There are so many worthy topics of conversation left unhad, simply because we haven't found the right people to have them with.
Looking forward to sparking conversations and adding food to my thoughts. If an article, an incomplete note, or a random rabbit hole here connects with something you are working on, please reach out. Let's talk.
